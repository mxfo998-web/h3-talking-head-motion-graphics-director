# Lifelike Talking-Head Performance

Use this reference for every visible speaker. Realism comes from motivated behavior with timing and recovery, not from constant random motion.

## 1. Causal performance cycle

For each meaningful beat, plan only the parts the scene needs:

1. **Attention target** — lens, co-host, product, monitor, world-space panel, surface graphic, or an actual scene event.
2. **Internal intent** — recognition, curiosity, concern, doubt, confidence, reassurance, resolve, or evaluation.
3. **Visible response** — eyes, lids, brow, cheeks, lip corners, jaw, breath, swallow, head, torso, hand, grip, or weight.
4. **Recovery** — return to the lens, listening, neutral breath, supported posture, hand rest, or preparation for the next phrase.

Start from a neutral baseline. Use one dominant response per short beat and at most one secondary response unless a denser style is explicitly requested. A motion graphic appearing beside a frozen person is a failure, but constant nodding, blinking, smiling, or fidgeting is also a failure.

## 2. Lens engagement and gaze

- Direct-to-camera speech is lens-first, with brief motivated departures rather than unwavering staring.
- Name every gaze target. The eyes usually lead a small head turn by a fraction of a beat.
- A world-space, surface-attached, or subject-linked graphic may motivate a short glance or one pointing gesture. The speaker acknowledges it, then returns to the lens before continuing the main claim.
- Audience-only screen-space overlays are not visible to the speaker; do not make the person look at them.
- Preserve small refocusing and micro-saccadic behavior without repeated left-right scanning.

## 3. Blinks and eye behavior

- Use isolated natural blinks near phrase boundaries, breath preparation, gaze shifts, cognitive release, or after effort; do not specify a universal rate.
- Avoid periodic blinking, repeated double blinks, frozen wide eyes, perfectly synchronized blink-and-nod events, or identical blinking among multiple people.
- Use squint, lid tension, or protective blinking only when light, wind, particles, dryness, surprise, or concentration justifies it.
- Keep eye highlights, iris direction, eyelid contact, and gaze continuity stable.

## 4. Face, mouth, and speech

- Build expressions from small components: brow compression or lift, cheek tension, one-sided lip-corner movement, jaw release, nostril change, swallow, or restrained smile.
- Use `onset → peak → partial release`; preserve slight asymmetry and avoid instant expression swaps, continuous smiling, beauty-ad posing, decorative eyebrow motion, and permanently parted lips.
- Mouth motion follows actual phonemes, syllable stress, and pauses. Include consonant closures, rounded vowels, brief lip seals, natural jaw amplitude, cheek/tongue support where visible, and a relaxed closed or neutral mouth during silence.
- Let inhalation prepare a long phrase. After a demanding sentence, allow a subtle exhale, swallow, lip reset, or posture release.
- Keep mouth, teeth, tongue, jaw, cheeks, facial identity, and skin texture continuous. Avoid tooth flicker, drifting lips, rubbery cheeks, waxy skin, or exaggerated mouth shapes.

## 5. Breathing, posture, and weight

- Breathing is subtle but continuous in the chest, abdomen, shoulders, collar, or fabric according to framing and wardrobe; do not exaggerate it.
- Seated speakers maintain believable pelvis/chair support and vary forearm pressure, shoulder balance, or torso lean slightly. Standing speakers maintain stable foot placement and make only motivated weight transfers or stance corrections.
- Head, neck, shoulders, torso, elbows, and hands must remain mechanically connected. After a reach, lean, laugh, or emphatic gesture, include inertia and recovery rather than an instant reset.
- Avoid frozen shoulders, perfectly symmetrical posture, repetitive swaying, and returning to the exact same pose after every sentence.

## 6. Hands, gestures, and props

- Give both hands explicit natural resting states that are not perfectly mirrored.
- A gesture begins slightly before or on the emphasized word, reaches one clear semantic peak, then settles and returns to rest.
- Choose a meaningful gesture: compact beat, open-palm explanation, restrained count, product demonstration, tracked point, or grip change. Do not wave continuously.
- Respect desk, microphone, clothing, frame edge, product, and panel location. Preserve hand dominance, finger count, joints, grip, contact point, and object continuity.
- Keep gesture density low during dense dialogue or difficult lip sync. Do not trigger nod, blink, brow lift, hand gesture, and torso lean all at once.

## 7. Person-to-graphic interaction

Classify the graphic before directing a reaction:

- **Screen-space overlay:** audience-only; speaker keeps lens engagement and does not look at it.
- **World-space panel / AR UI:** speaker may briefly glance, indicate, or orient toward it; preserve perspective, occlusion, and plausible reach.
- **Surface-attached graphic:** speaker may reference the physical surface, with gaze and hand matching the same point.
- **Subject-linked callout:** the speaker usually ignores the graphic unless it represents an interface they can perceive.

Use the interaction sequence `gaze preparation → graphic entrance → semantic landing → one human response → graphic hold/exit → gaze and body recovery`. Do not let the graphic control every facial movement or make the person chase several panels.

## 8. Genre calibration

| Context | Performance profile |
| --- | --- |
| Technology / product | Curious recognition to confident explanation; precise compact gestures; brief panel glance; no fixed sales smile |
| Finance / policy | Lens-first restraint; small skeptical or cautionary facial beat; disciplined hands; grounded posture |
| Medical / science | Calm authority with human empathy; measured pace; open-palm clarification; no cheerful reaction to risk |
| Property / lifestyle | Conversational warmth; occasional environment glance; relaxed asymmetry; avoid repetitive agent-style nodding |
| Industrial / energy | Grounded stance; deliberate gaze to equipment/data; compact controlled gesture; visible physical confidence without stiffness |
| Education | Deliberate eye contact; paced explanation; semantic hand shapes; brief comprehension-check expression |
| Interview / podcast | Turn-taking gaze; low gesture density; active listening; natural breathing and restrained backchannels |

## 9. Complexity and framing

- Close-ups need smaller expression and gesture amplitudes; medium shots can show torso and compact hand action; wider shots need readable posture and weight.
- Fixed camera means the camera remains static, not that the person becomes static.
- When text accuracy or effects are complex, reduce decorative UI and body-action density before sacrificing face, mouth, gaze, breath, or hand continuity.
- If the framing hides feet, fine finger detail, or a contact point, do not claim precise control that cannot be seen.

## 10. Human-realism audit

Before delivery verify:

- every gaze departure has a target and return;
- blinks are contextual, sparse, and non-periodic;
- expressions transition and partially release;
- phoneme-level mouth behavior stops in silence;
- breath prepares and resolves phrases;
- posture carries support, weight, and recovery;
- gestures are motivated, peak once, and return to rest;
- hands, fingers, props, face, teeth, eyes, hair, clothing, and skin stay continuous;
- the person remains alive during quiet spans without random motion;
- graphic interaction matches its coordinate space;
- no decorative effect outranks the speaker's eyes and mouth.

If any answer is unclear, simplify the performance or effect plan and write the missing target, cause, timing, or recovery explicitly.
